✦ Cultural Tips

  • Remove your shoes before entering homes, temples, and certain small businesses.
  • Greet with the "Wai" (pressing your palms together with a slight bow) to show respect.
  • Never touch someone's head, as it is considered the most sacred part of the body.
  • Point with an open hand rather than a single index finger to remain polite.
  • Dress modestly at temples by ensuring your shoulders and knees are fully covered.
  • Show absolute respect for the Royal Family, as speaking ill of them is strictly against the law.
  • Add polite particles ("Krap" for men, "Ka" for women) to the end of your sentences.
  • Share meals family-style, taking only small portions from the central dishes at a time.
  • Use your right hand to pass money or food, as the left hand is traditionally considered unclean.
  • Keep your feet pointed away from people, monks, and religious objects, as feet are seen as the lowest part of the body.
  • Maintain a "cool heart" (Jai Yen) by avoiding public outbursts or losing your temper.
  • Stand completely still if you hear the National Anthem playing in public spaces, which typically occurs at 8 AM and 6 PM.
  • Respect monastic rules: Women should never physically touch a Buddhist monk or hand items directly to them.
  • Embrace the "Mai pen rai" (no worries) mindset, keeping a flexible and relaxed attitude toward daily inconveniences.
  • Eat with a spoon and fork: Hold the spoon in your right hand and use the left fork to push food onto it. Chopsticks are for noodle soups.
  • Dispose of toilet paper in the provided bin rather than flushing it, and utilize the bidet sprayer (the "bum gun") instead.
  • Leave a small tip (20–50 baht) for good service, though standard Western-style tipping is not mandatory.
  • Treat Buddha images with deep reverence; never pose disrespectfully with them or purchase them merely as casual home decor.
  • Keep public displays of affection modest; holding hands is perfectly acceptable, but overt kissing is frowned upon.
  • The "Golden Rule" of utensils: Hold the spoon in your right hand and the fork in your left. Use the fork only to push food onto the spoon—never put the fork in your mouth.
  • Chopsticks have a specific place: Reserve chopsticks only for noodle soups; they are rarely used for rice or stir-fried dishes.
  • Wait for the host: In traditional settings, it is polite to wait for the eldest person at the table to take the first bite before you begin your meal.
  • Mind your trash: If you are eating street food, look for the designated bin. Never leave trash on the table, even if you see others do it.
  • The "Bum Gun" etiquette: If you see a small sprayer next to the toilet, it is for personal hygiene. Aim away from the floor and walls to avoid making a mess for the next person!
  • Shared dishes are the norm: When eating with others, you are expected to share. Everyone usually orders different dishes for the table, and you take small portions of each onto your own plate of rice.
  • Respect the bill: In social settings, it is common for the person who invited the group or the senior person to pay for the meal. Do not insist on "splitting the check", this can be seen as awkward.
